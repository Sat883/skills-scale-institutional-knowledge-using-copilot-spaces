## Retrospective Agenda

### 1. Set the Stage (5 minutes)
**Purpose**: Create psychological safety and focus

- Welcome participants and state the retrospective's purpose
- Remind the team of the retrospective prime directive: *"Regardless of what we discover, we understand and believe that everyone did the best job they could, given what they knew at the time, their skills and abilities, the resources available, and the situation at hand."*
- Review the agenda and timeboxes
- Establish ground rules: respect, confidentiality, participation, constructive feedback

## Facilitator Tips

### Creating Psychological Safety
- Use anonymous input tools for sensitive topics
- Model vulnerability by sharing your own challenges first
- Ensure everyone's voice is heard; actively invite quieter members
- Focus on processes and systems, not individuals
- Reinforce that mistakes are learning opportunities

### Keeping Energy High
- Use diverse activities and formats
- Keep discussions focused and timeboxed
- Celebrate wins and progress early in the retro
- Use humor appropriately
- Take breaks for longer retrospectives

### Driving Actionable Outcomes
- Focus on what the team can control
- Make action items specific, measurable, and time-bound
- Assign clear ownership
- Limit to 2-3 high-impact actions
- Follow up consistently on commitments

### Avoiding Common Pitfalls
- Don't skip retros, even when "nothing happened"
- Don't let the same people dominate discussion
- Don't create action items without owners or due dates
- Don't ignore recurring themes
- Don't blame individuals for systemic issues

### Varying Retrospective Formats
- **Sailboat**: Team as a sailboat - wind (what helps), anchors (what slows), rocks (risks), island (goal)
- **4Ls**: Liked, Learned, Lacked, Longed For
- **Starfish**: More of, Less of, Keep doing, Start doing, Stop doing
- **Mad/Sad/Glad**: Categorize emotions about the sprint
- **Speed Car**: What accelerates us, what slows us down
